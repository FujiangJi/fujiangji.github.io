  • [1] Ji, F.; Yang, J.; Townsend, P. A.; Zheng, T.; Kovach, K. R.; Yu, T.; Yang, R.; Liu, M.; Chen, M.

    Robust hyperspectral reconstruction from satellite and airborne observations via a deep hierarchical fusion network across heterogeneous scenarios
    Remote Sensing of Environment2026, 338, 115385First AuthorJIF 11.4 · 2024JCR Q1 · Environmental SciencesPlant traits & diversity

    High spatial resolution hyperspectral imagery (HR-HSI) is essential for fine-scale ecological and environmental monitoring, yet current spaceborne sensors are constrained by a trade-off between spectral and spatial resolution. This study develops a deep learning fusion framework that reconstructs HR-HSI by integrating low-resolution hyperspectral imagery from NASA's EMIT with high-resolution multispectral imagery from PlanetScope. The model was evaluated across three ecologically distinct landscapes in the western United States using airborne AVIRIS-NG and AVIRIS-3 imagery as reference data. The proposed framework consistently outperformed seven peer state-of-the-art fusion models, achieving a best spectral angle mapper (SAM) of 2.64 and a root mean squared error (RMSE) of 0.0267. A sensor inter-calibration pipeline ensured radiometric consistency between EMIT and PlanetScope, improving the reliability of the fusion inputs.

  • [2] Wang, W.; Lu, B.; Li, Y.; Ji, F.*

    Multiscale Morphology-Based Detection of Shoreline Change Hotspots from Aerial Imagery Under Fluctuating Water Levels
    Remote Sensing2026, 18(8), 1148Corresponding AuthorJIF 4.3 · 2025JCR Q1 · Geosciences, MultidisciplinaryJCR Q2 · Remote SensingOther research & applications

    This study tested whether shoreline shape and displacement can identify erosion and accretion hotspots despite changing lake levels. Along 125 km of southwestern Lake Michigan shoreline, 2010 and 2020 aerial imagery was evaluated with Fréchet, modified Euclidean, and combined Union distances at 100 m, 500 m, and 1 km scales. The combined measure improved hotspot detection over uncalibrated transect-based comparisons at 500 m and 1 km. Performance depended on local shoreline conditions, and the 100 m scale remained sensitive to water-level effects. The framework offers calibration-free regional screening while retaining the need for water-level correction in detailed local assessments.

  • [3] Wang, W.; Lu, B.; Li, Y.; Ji, F.*

    A Quantitative Assessment of the Inconsistency Between Waterbody Segmentation and Shoreline Positioning in Deep Learning Models
    Geomatics2026, 6(1), 21Corresponding AuthorJIF 3.7 · 2025JCR Q2 · Remote SensingJCR Q2 · Geography, PhysicalOther research & applications

    This study evaluates the inconsistency between deep learning-based waterbody segmentation and shoreline positioning using aerial imagery from the Great Lakes region. Despite strong pixel-level segmentation accuracy (>98% accuracy, >90% IoU) across five benchmark models tested on 20,689 NAIP images, shoreline positioning accuracy was substantially lower and varied significantly by landscape type—with poorest results in wetlands and urban areas. The shoreline-to-water ratio emerged as the dominant predictor of positioning accuracy, while shoreline complexity and water color showed weaker, context-dependent effects. These findings highlight the need for shoreline-specific evaluation metrics rather than relying solely on conventional segmentation benchmarks when applying deep learning to coastal and lake boundary detection tasks.

  • [4] Ji, F.; Zheng, T.; Shiklomanov, A. N.; Yang, R.; Townsend, P. A.; Li, F.; Hao, D.; Dashti, H.; Kovach, K. R.; You, H.; Zhou, J.; Chen, M.

    Tracking seasonal variability in plant traits from spaceborne PRISMA and NEON AOP across forest types and ecoregions
    Remote Sensing of Environment2026, 333, 115149First AuthorJIF 11.4 · 2024JCR Q1 · Environmental SciencesPlant traits & diversity

    This study combined PRISMA imaging spectroscopy with NEON airborne observations to map seasonal changes in chlorophyll, carotenoids, equivalent water thickness, and nitrogen across 11 forest sites in the contiguous United States. PLSR models first produced 1 m NEON trait maps, which were aggregated to 30 m and used to train site-level models with PRISMA reflectance and PRISMA-derived leaf area index as predictors. Validation yielded R² of 0.78–0.88 and NRMSE of 5.4–8.4%. Chlorophyll and carotenoids followed bell-shaped seasonal patterns, water thickness generally declined, and nitrogen varied less strongly. The analysis also identified regional climatic controls on seasonality and soil-related controls on spatial variation.

  • [5] Ji, F.; Li, F.; Dashti, H.; Hao, D.; Townsend, P. A.; Zheng, T.; You, H.; Chen, M.

    Leveraging transfer learning and leaf spectroscopy for leaf trait prediction with broad spatial, species, and temporal applicability
    Remote Sensing of Environment2025, 326, 114818First AuthorJIF 11.4 · 2024JCR Q1 · Environmental SciencesPlant traits & diversity

    A hybrid neural network was pretrained with radiative transfer simulations and fine-tuned with measured leaf spectra to estimate chlorophyll, carotenoids, leaf mass per area, and equivalent water thickness. Tests using three datasets from the United States and Europe evaluated prediction across locations, plant functional types, and seasons. The framework improved accuracy and transferability relative to PLSR, Gaussian process regression, and radiative transfer model inversion. Incorporating leaf-optics knowledge before fine-tuning helped the model make effective use of limited observations in new environments.

  • [6] Ji, F.*; Li, F.; Hao, D.; Shiklomanov, A. N.; Yang, X.; Townsend, P. A.; Dashti, H.; Nakaji, T.; Kovach, K. R.; Liu, H.; Luo, M.; Chen, M.

    Unveiling the transferability of PLSR models for leaf trait estimation: lessons from a comprehensive analysis with a novel global dataset
    New Phytologist2024, 243(1), 111–131Co-First Author · Corresponding AuthorJIF 8.7ESI Top 1% Highly CitedPlant traits & diversity

    Using 47,393 leaf spectral records spanning more than 700 species, eight plant functional types, and 101 sites, this study tested whether PLSR predictions of chlorophyll, carotenoids, water content, and leaf mass per area transfer beyond their training conditions. Prediction accuracy declined for new locations (R² = 0.12–0.49), seasons (0.15–0.42), and plant functional types (0.25–0.56). Random cross-validation therefore gave an overly optimistic picture of performance in new domains. Training samples with greater spectral diversity improved transferability, supporting more representative sampling and explicit out-of-domain validation for large-scale trait mapping.

  • [7] Ji, F.; Meng, J.; Cheng, Z.; Fang, H.; Wang, Y.

    Crop Yield Estimation at Field Scales by Assimilating Time Series of Sentinel-2 Data into a Modified CASA-WOFOST Coupled Model
    IEEE Trans. Geosci. Remote Sens.2022, 60, 4400914 (pp. 1–14)First AuthorJIF 9.4Agricultural remote sensing

    A modified CASA–WOFOST framework combined Sentinel-2 time series with crop growth modeling to estimate field-scale wheat yield at two adjacent farms in China. CASA, the coupled model, and WOFOST achieved R² values of 0.64, 0.84, and 0.86, respectively, with RMSE of 948.32, 792.11, and 623.64 kg ha⁻¹. The coupled model improved on CASA, particularly under uneven terrain, while retaining accuracy close to WOFOST at much lower computational cost. Processing one wheat season took 48 minutes for the coupled model, compared with 37 minutes for CASA and more than a day for WOFOST.

  • [8] Ji, F.; Meng, J.; Fang, H.

    Study on Soybean Yield Estimation Using the Coupled CASA and WOFOST Model
    Remote Sensing Technology and Application2020, 35(2), 406–415First AuthorIn Chinese with English AbstractAgricultural remote sensing

    At Shuangshan Farm in Heilongjiang, China, this study combined the CASA light-use-efficiency model with dry-matter partitioning from WOFOST to estimate field-scale soybean yield. HJ-1A/B satellite images spanning the growing season were used to reconstruct a daily NDVI time series for continuous crop monitoring. Compared with CASA alone, the coupled framework improved agreement with measured yields, increasing R² from 0.66853 to 0.84472. By replacing a fixed harvest index with mechanistic crop growth and allocation processes, the approach retained the computational efficiency of CASA while improving the accuracy and reliability of yield estimation for precision agriculture.

  • [9] You, H.; Ji, F.; Park, T.; Radeloff, V. C.; Hurtt, G.; Jiang, M.; Chen, M.

    Episodic changes in global forest edges in the 21st century
    Nature Communications2026Co-AuthorJIF 18.1 · 2025Forest fragmentation
    Forest-edge mapping tutorial →

    Seventy percent of global forests lie within 1 km of an edge, yet their temporal dynamics remain poorly characterized. By developing high spatial-resolution (0.00025°) global forest edge maps for 2000–2020, this study finds a 24 million km (7%) decline in edge length. During 2015–2020, edge length falls while forest area slightly grows, driven by patch merging in temperate and boreal regions and by tropical fragmentation; in contrast, countries such as Laos, Liberia, India, and China gain edges from new forest patches. These episodic changes closely track policy-driven land management, including logging bans, agricultural expansion, and reforestation. Given the critical role of forest edges in biodiversity and microclimate regulation, the results highlight contrasting biome patterns and the need for adaptive policies balancing conservation, carbon storage, and land use.

  • [10] Smith, W. K.; Villarreal, M. L.; Lauria, C.; Rutherford, W. A.; Herrmann, S.; Scholl, V.; Howell, A.; Javadian, M.; Ji, F.; Zhang, F.; Burgess, M. A.; Kokaly, R.; Poulter, B.; & Reed, S. C.

    Experimental evidence of a biological soil crust degradation climate warming amplification feedback
    Communications Earth & Environment2026, 7, 775Co-AuthorJIF 9.3 · 2025Dryland biocrusts & climate

    Biological soil crusts—photosynthetic communities of cyanobacteria, lichens, and mosses living on soil surfaces—cover roughly 12% of the global land surface, yet their role in climate mitigation remains uncertain. This study integrated multiscale remote sensing observations within a two-decade climate manipulation experiment in Castle Valley, Utah. Sustained warming and altered monsoonal rainfall drove a major shift in biocrust community composition, including a ~27% decrease in the fractional cover of late-successional mosses and a ~36% increase in early-successional, lightly-pigmented cyanobacteria. For every 10% increase in the relative cover of lightly-pigmented cyanobacteria, the authors observed a ~6.3% reduction in photosynthetic potential, a ~14.1% increase in surface brightness, a ~15.2% decrease in surface moisture, and a ~0.3 °C increase in clear-sky daytime surface temperature. These results provide experimental evidence of a biocrust degradation climate warming amplification feedback that is currently missing from process-based models, suggesting current projections may underestimate the rate of warming across global drylands.

  • [11] Liu, H.; Xiao, J.; Hao, D.; Li, F.; Ji, F.; Chen, M.

    Importance of viewing angle: Hotspot effect improves the ability of satellites to track terrestrial photosynthesis
    Remote Sensing of Environment2025, 317, 114492Co-AuthorJIF 11.4 · 2024JCR Q1 · Environmental SciencesPlant traits & diversity

    This study examined how viewing geometry affects the relationship between gross primary productivity and NIRvP, the product of near-infrared vegetation reflectance and photosynthetically active radiation. DSCOVR/EPIC observations enabled comparison of near-hotspot geometry, where viewing and illumination directions align, with nadir geometry. Hotspot-based NIRvP tracked productivity variations more closely across ecosystems from diurnal to daily scales. MODIS observations and SCOPE model simulations supported the result. The findings point to sun-tracking observation geometries as a useful way to improve satellite monitoring of terrestrial photosynthesis.

  • [12] Li, F.; Zhu, Q.; Yuan, K.; Ji, F.; Paul, A.; Lee, P.; Radeloff, V. C.; Chen, M.

    Projecting Large Fires in the Western US With an Interpretable and Accurate Hybrid Machine Learning Method
    Earth's Future2024, 12(10), e2024EF004588Co-AuthorJIF 8.5Other research & applications

    A hybrid machine learning model combined fuel availability, fuel flammability, and human suppression to predict large fires in the western United States. Its F1 score was 0.846 ± 0.012, outperforming process-based fire danger indices and four other machine learning models by up to 40% and 9%, respectively.     Structural complexity biases vegetation greenness measures
    Nature Ecology & Evolution2023, 7(11), 1790–1798Co-AuthorJIF 17.1 · 2025Plant traits & diversity
    Media 1 NASA Medium Phys.org Free version

    Vegetation indices can show higher greenness in the US Corn Belt than in the Amazon despite the Amazon's greater leaf area. This study traced the discrepancy to canopy structure: complex forests cast more shadows than relatively uniform crops, reducing observed greenness when the viewing direction differs from solar illumination. Converting Amazon forest to cropland could consequently increase vegetation indices while decreasing leaf area. The results show why changes in greenness, and their relationship with solar-induced fluorescence, require attention to structural and viewing-angle effects when interpreting global vegetation trends.

    Aboveground biomass estimation of late-stage maize based on the WOFOST model and UAV observations
    Journal of Remote Sensing (遥感学报)2020, 24(11), 1403–1418Co-AuthorIn Chinese with English AbstractAgricultural remote sensing

    To avoid spectral saturation during late maize growth, this study estimated late-season aboveground biomass by assimilating early-season UAV observations into WOFOST with an ensemble Kalman filter, then continuing the crop simulation without late-season image assimilation. Multispectral imagery also supplied spatial information on available soil nutrients to parameterize the model. Against field measurements, the proposed approach achieved R² = 0.86 and RMSE = 2216.79 kg ha⁻¹, compared with R² = 0.45 and RMSE = 4254.30 kg ha⁻¹ for assimilation throughout the growing season. This illustrates how early observations can support biomass estimates after optical signals become less sensitive to crop growth.

    Comparison of Machine Learning Regression Algorithms for Cotton Leaf Area Index Retrieval Using Sentinel-2 Spectral Bands
    Applied Sciences2019, 9(7), 1459Co-AuthorJIF 2.9 · 2025JCR Q2 · Engineering, MultidisciplinaryAgricultural remote sensing

    This study compared ANN, SVR, Gaussian process regression, random forest, and gradient-boosted regression trees for cotton leaf area index retrieval from Sentinel-2 bands over three growing seasons in northwestern China.     Plant functional trait estimation from PlanetScope–EMIT reconstructed high-resolution hyperspectral imagery reveals spectral-spatial fidelity trade-offs

    Hyperspectral reflectance reveals climate change–driven successional regression in dryland biocrust communities

    Under Review — Global Change BiologyFirst Author · Co-Corresponding Author2026Dryland biocrusts & climate

    Manuscript under review. This study estimates the fractional cover of all major biocrust functional types from mixed hyperspectral pixels using spectral unmixing and deep learning, with visible-range pigment absorption bands as the dominant predictors alongside red-edge and shortwave infrared contributions.     New opportunities for global monitoring of vulnerable biological soil crust

    In PreparationFirst Author · Co-Corresponding Author2026Dryland biocrusts & climate

    Manuscript in preparation. Biological soil crusts cover roughly 12% of Earth’s terrestrial surface and act as ecosystem engineers of the world’s drylands, yet they remain effectively invisible to the satellite records and Earth system models used to monitor the land surface. This perspective argues that a new generation of hyperspectral imaging spectrometers can resolve diagnostic signals of pigments, hydration, organic matter, and mineral substrates across the visible-to-shortwave-infrared spectrum.   • [R8] Ji, F.*; You, H.; Smith, W. K.; Townsend, P. A.; Xu, X.; Zheng, T.; Hao, D.; Li, F.; Liu, S.; Chen, M.

    Forest edges impose a global syndrome on canopy function

    In PreparationCo-First Author · Co-Corresponding Author2026Forest fragmentation

    Manuscript in preparation. Using NASA’s EMIT spaceborne imaging spectrometer, this study analyzes 49 hyperspectral indices representing ten canopy functional groups across 2,371 peak-growing-season scenes worldwide, covering roughly 1.35 billion forest pixels. Distance from a forest edge structures canopy function along a coherent global syndrome: canopies are drier, more stressed, more structurally and chemically defended, and more spectrally heterogeneous near edges, but greener, wetter, more chlorophyll-rich, and more productive toward interiors. Edge influence is depth-ordered—canopy water and spectral heterogeneity recover within hundreds of meters to about a kilometer, while structural, photoprotective, and defensive chemical signals persist for several kilometers.
